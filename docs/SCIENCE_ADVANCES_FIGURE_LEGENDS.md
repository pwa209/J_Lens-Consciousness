# Main figure legends

## Figure 1. One study links human access geometry to causal computational tests.

**a,** Human EEG and controlled artificial-network arms meet only at a common
four-metric Jacobian geometry; human recordings do not train the baseline
models. **b,** Five architectures instantiate feedforward, recurrent,
local-sufficiency, capacity-limited shared-state, and unlimited shared-state
accounts. **c,** The inferential chain separates characterization, held-out
human-guided adaptation, intervention, external transfer, and synthesis.
**d,** Outcome-blind preprocessing, fold-isolated transforms, frozen baseline
lineages, and held-out geometry endpoints limit circularity. This is one study
with linked analytical stages, not two separately labelled experiments.

## Figure 2. Human access geometry is dataset-dependent and adds no incremental prediction.

**a,** Cross-fitted Access Index differences over peri-stimulus time for the
Gabor discovery dataset and the Kronemer and somatosensory replication datasets;
bands are uncertainty intervals and the shaded region marks the primary window.
**b,** Participant-level primary-window contrasts with dataset means and 95%
confidence intervals. The positive Gabor effect did not generalize uniformly.
**c,** Dataset-specific standardized contrasts for gain, broadcast, persistence,
and concentration, expressed in discovery-sample standard-deviation units.
**d,** Held-out area under the curve for conventional predictors alone and with
Jacobian metrics added. The mean incremental AUC was approximately 0.0001
(directional sign-flip p = 0.501).

## Figure 3. Matched architectures exhibit distinct and causally relevant geometries.

**a,** Presence accuracy across difficulty bins for the five independently
trained architectures. **b,** Step-resolved mean Jacobian gain. **c,** Accuracy
loss after intervention in the high-gain subspace compared with matched random
subspaces; points and intervals summarize paired seeds. **d,** Accuracy-matched
machine fingerprints for the four geometry metrics. Colors identify
architectures consistently throughout the article.

## Figure 4. Baseline human-machine convergence does not privilege a capacity-limited workspace.

**a,** Held-out human-machine root-mean-square geometry distance by architecture
(lower values indicate closer correspondence). Feedforward ranked first at
baseline. **b,** Human and machine four-metric profiles. **c,** Prespecified
capacity-limited-minus-unlimited contrasts with 90% confidence intervals and
equivalence bounds. The conditions were equivalent for gain, broadcast, and
concentration, but not persistence. **d,** Leave-one-dataset-out standardized
distance and cosine similarity show that cross-dataset correspondence is
heterogeneous. Together these results do not support the claim that a shared
capacity bottleneck is necessary for human-like geometry.

## Figure 5. Human-guided adaptation does not improve held-out geometry.

**a,** Outcome-isolated design: a lower-level 150–300-ms EEG temporal
representational-similarity target adapts architecture-specific dynamics while
the encoder and task heads remain frozen; participant-held-out geometry and task
retention are evaluation-only. A jointly time-permuted target supplies the
matched sham. **b,** Held-out RMS distance from random initialization through
task training, human-target adaptation, and sham adaptation. **c,** Human-specific
gain over sham for all runs and complete-case seeds. No architecture showed a
Holm-corrected benefit. **d,** Task-accuracy change versus relative parameter
displacement. Crosses mark human-target runs outside the ±0.02 retention gate;
17 of 500 human-target and 17 of 500 sham runs failed. All-runs,
gate-compliant, and complete-case analyses agreed on the corrected null result.

## Figure 6. Causal and transfer tests define the boundary of the adaptation result.

**a,** Targeted-minus-random intervention drops before and after human-guided
adaptation. **b,** Alignment gain when Gabor-adapted models are evaluated against
the two Kronemer and two somatosensory contrasts; uncorrected cells are
descriptive and do not establish general transfer. **c,** Prespecified evidence
map separating baseline fit, human-target adaptation, sham specificity,
external transfer, causal change, and task retention; no composite score is
computed. **d,** Architecture means and seed-level values for held-out alignment
gain versus causal-specificity change. The integrated pattern is a constraint
and falsification result: baseline resemblance, acquireability, transfer, and
causal preservation are separable requirements.
